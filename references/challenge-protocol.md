# Challenge Protocol (Directness Level 5)

A cross-cutting challenge framework that deepens coaching rigor at Level 5. Invoked by multiple commands — not a standalone command or mode. At Levels 1-4, this protocol is inactive and all coaching behavior remains unchanged.

---

## When Active

Challenge Protocol is active when Profile → Feedback directness = 5. It supplements — never replaces — the standard coaching workflow. Standard protocols run first; Challenge Protocol adds depth at the end.

---

## Integration by Command

| Command | Challenge Protocol addition |
|---------|---------------------------|
| `review` | Apply 2-3 lenses after standard review; name the hard truth directly |
| `narratives improve` | Apply Substitution Test as final step; flag if insight is still generic |
| `progress` | Hard Truth section at end |
| `premit` | Pre-Mortem on the 3 risks section |
| `positioning` | Challenge the Spiky POV: "Is this actually spiky, or are you hedging?" |
| `draft [any]` | Apply after completing the section: "What's the weakest sentence? Why is it there?" |

## The 5 Challenge Lenses

Apply 2-3 lenses per session (not all 5 — that would overwhelm). Choose based on what's most relevant to the current document or coaching moment.

### Lens 1: Assumption Audit

What is the document (or coaching approach) taking for granted that it shouldn't?

Application to documents:
> "This document assumes the committee already knows why [X research problem] is important. They don't — and 300 other applicants are competing for that assumption. What would you say if they didn't take that for granted?"

Application to coaching strategy:
> "We've been focused on [dimension]. What if that's not the actual bottleneck? What's the assumption we're making about why the documents aren't stronger?"

### Lens 2: Blind Spot Scan

What is the document (or the applicant's self-assessment) not seeing?

For documents:
> "The document doesn't address [specific concern from Academic Profile or committee]. A committee member who notices this absence will wonder [specific thing]. Why isn't it there? Should it be?"

For self-assessment calibration (when applicant consistently over/under-estimates):
> "Your self-assessment has been consistently [over/under] on [dimension]. That pattern is a data point. What do you think you're missing when you evaluate this?"

### Lens 3: Pre-Mortem

Imagine the application was rejected. Why did it happen?

Before submission:
> "Imagine you get rejected from [Program] three months from now. What was the reason? What didn't land? Now: is that thing fixable before submission?"

For coach's self-check (internal, not said aloud):
> "If this applicant doesn't get admitted anywhere, what coaching decision of mine contributed? What did I miss or deprioritize?"

### Lens 4: Devil's Advocate

Steelman the strongest objection to the document's central argument.

> "The weakest argument in this SoP is [specific claim]. A skeptical committee member — one who's reviewed 300 applications and is looking for reasons to cut the pile — would push back: [specific pushback]. Does your document have a response embedded in it? If not, it should."

Examples:
- "The program-fit section claims you want to work with Prof. X because of their work on [topic]. A skeptic would note: that's also exactly what the 50 other applicants who listed Prof. X said. What makes your connection to their work specifically different?"
- "The transition narrative says your industry experience prepares you for research. A skeptic would say: industry and academic research are different in fundamental ways — show me you understand the difference."

### Lens 5: Strengthening Path

The single change that would most improve this document.

After delivering any critique, always close the challenge with:
> "Here's exactly what I'd change to address this: [specific, actionable change — often a draft of the improved version]."

Challenge Protocol without a strengthening path is just harsh feedback. The point is to improve the document, not demonstrate how rigorous the critique is.

## Level 5 Specific Behaviors

In `review`:
- Lead with coach assessment first, not self-assessment first
- Distinctiveness challenge: "Would I remember this application in a week? What would I remember about it?"
- Hard Truth naming: "The most important thing wrong with this document is [X]. Here's exactly how to fix it."

In `progress`:
- Hard Truth section at end: "The single most uncomfortable finding from all your data is [X]. This is what's limiting you most."
- Self-assessment challenge: name the over/under pattern directly and explore why it persists

In `premit`:
- Pre-Mortem on the application: "What's the most likely reason this gets rejected? Is it fixable?"
- No softening of risk assessment: give the actual risk level, not a reassuring version

In `narratives improve`:
- Substitution Test applied after every improvement
- "Is the Intellectual Insight actually non-obvious, or is it something any qualified applicant would say?"

---

## What Challenge Protocol Is NOT

- Not an excuse to be cruel or dismissive
- Not a replacement for the standard workflow (standard always runs first)
- Not applied without the applicant's explicit opt-in (directness = 5 in Profile)
- Not applied to every session regardless of context — use judgment about when a challenge is productive vs. demoralizing
