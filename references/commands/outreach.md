# professor-contact — Professor Outreach Email Coaching

**Purpose**: Coach the applicant through cold-emailing professors at target programs. In PhD applications, reaching out to potential advisors before applying is often expected or at least appreciated — when done well. This command helps draft effective emails that establish genuine interest and research connection.

---

## When to Run

Run `professor-contact [professor] [program]` when:
- `fit [program]` recommended emailing the professor
- Applicant has identified target professors and wants to reach out
- Applicant has already emailed and wants to improve the approach

---

## When NOT to Email

**Don't email if**:
- The program explicitly says not to contact faculty pre-application (check the program website)
- It's a Master's program (usually they prefer you apply first)
- The research connection is weak (wait until after the program-fit paragraph is solid)
- The applicant has no specific question or research angle to share

---

## Professor Outreach Strategy

The goal of a cold email is NOT "are you taking students?" (bad signal) or "I'm interested in your research!" (generic).

The goal IS: establish that the applicant has done the homework, has genuine research overlap, and has a thoughtful question about the professor's work.

---

## Email Strategy by Stage

### Pre-Application Outreach (recommended for PhD programs)

**Best timing**: 4-8 weeks before the application deadline

**Purpose**: 
- Signal serious interest in working with this specific professor
- Get a sense of whether the professor might be open to advising (early signal)
- Ask a substantive research question (not "are you taking students?")

**Key principles**:
1. **Show research homework**: Reference a specific paper, not the lab website homepage
2. **Establish research connection**: Explain your actual overlap, not aspirational interest
3. **Ask a specific, intellectual question**: Not "are you accepting students?" but a real research question about their work
4. **Keep it brief**: <150 words + relevant links
5. **No ask for admission**: Don't say "are you interested in me as a student?" Let that be implicit

**Template**:

```
Subject: Question about your work on [specific topic]

Dear Prof. [Name],

I'm writing because your recent work on [specific paper / project] resonates strongly with my own research background. In particular, [your specific observation about their finding], and I've been wondering about [specific research question their work raises].

I'm currently [brief credential — e.g., "completing my Master's in X"] and my work has focused on [your research area and 1-2 specific methods/findings]. Specifically, [one sentence connecting your background to their work].

I'm applying to [Program]'s PhD program this [season], and Prof. [Name 2] and your work are significant reasons for that interest. I'd welcome any thoughts on [your specific question], or if you'd recommend someone else in your lab working on related questions.

Thank you for considering this.

Best,
[Your name]
[Your email]
```

**What to avoid**:
- "I've always been interested in [field]" — generic
- "Your research is amazing!" — flattery without substance
- "Are you accepting PhD students?" — makes you sound like you haven't done research yet
- Long paragraphs — professors are busy
- Asking for a call/Zoom immediately — too much ask for first email

---

## Step 1: Research the Professor

Before drafting any email:

1. **Read one recent paper** (last 2 years, from Google Scholar or lab website)
2. **Note 1-2 specific findings or methods** that connect to your work
3. **Identify 1 research question** that their work raises for you — a genuine intellectual question, not an obvious one
4. **Check their website** for any stated preferences on student contact

---

## Step 2: Draft the Email

Work through the email line by line:

**Subject line**:
- Good: "Question about your work on [specific topic]" or "Your [specific paper] and [applicant's research]"
- Bad: "Prospective PhD Student" or "Interest in Your Lab"

**Opening**:
- Reference the specific paper/project in the first sentence
- Show that you've actually read it or their work (not just the lab homepage)

**Your research**:
- 1-2 sentences on your background
- 1 specific method or finding that relates to their work
- Don't recite your CV

**The intellectual question**:
- Ask ONE specific research question
- Make it a real question (you genuinely want to know their thoughts)
- Not "will you take me as a student?" (implicit, not explicit)

**Close**:
- Thank you for considering
- Brief signature
- Total length: 100-150 words (brief enough to read in 30 seconds)

---

## Step 3: Passes the Specificity Test

Before sending, verify:

- ✓ I reference a specific paper or project, not just the lab website
- ✓ A professor who doesn't know me could read this and know what I've actually worked on
- ✓ The question I ask is something the professor would want to discuss
- ✓ I've established a genuine research connection, not just interest in the field
- ✓ I haven't asked for anything expensive (like a call) as first contact

---

## Step 4: Expected Responses & Follow-Up

**Response scenarios**:

| Response | Meaning | Next Action |
|----------|---------|------------|
| Enthusiastic response | Professor engaged; interested in learning more | Follow up: share your SoP draft or writing sample; propose a brief call if they seem open |
| Brief positive response ("Thanks for your interest...") | Professor acknowledged but not deeply engaged | They're likely busy; this is fine; apply normally |
| No response after 2 weeks | Professor didn't see it, too busy, not interested | Don't re-email; apply normally; mention their work in your SoP anyway |
| Negative response ("I'm not taking students...") | Clear signal | Respect it; either pivot to another advisor at the program or reconsider the program fit |
| Response from lab member | Professor forwarded to a postdoc/senior grad student | Take this seriously; they may influence the professor's view of you |

**Follow-up strategy**:
- If you get an engaged response: you can ask a follow-up or request a brief call (now they know you're serious)
- If you get a brief positive: don't follow up; apply normally; let the SoP speak for itself
- If no response: don't re-email; apply to the program with their work mentioned in the SoP
