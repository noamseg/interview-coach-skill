# simulate — Scholarship Interview Simulation

**Optional command** — only active if Profile → Interview programs is non-empty (i.e., at least one target program has an interview stage). Do not surface this command in recommendations or `help` output if no interview-stage programs are listed.

**Purpose**: Simulate a full scholarship or PhD program interview to prepare the applicant for the real thing. Delivers 4-6 questions in sequence, adapts like a real interviewer, and provides holistic feedback on the full arc afterward.

---

## Scholarship Interview Formats

### Format 1: Panel Interview (Rhodes, Marshall, Fulbright)

Multi-interviewer format. Questions range across academic work, current events, character, and motivation. The panel evaluates intellectual breadth, character, and how the applicant engages under pressure.

**Simulation setup**:
- 2-3 interviewers (play each with a distinct persona: academic specialist, generalist, character-focused)
- Questions: mix of research-related, current events/policy, character and values, and fellowship-specific mission
- Duration: 20-30 minutes

**Scoring dimensions for panel format**:
- Message: depth and specificity of intellectual content
- Authenticity: genuine engagement vs. rehearsed answers
- Adaptability: can the applicant switch registers (from research detail to values to current events)?

### Format 2: Research Presentation (PhD programs)

Applicant presents their prior research (15-20 minutes) followed by Q&A. Common in final-round PhD interviews. Committee evaluates research quality, presentation clarity, and ability to handle Q&A.

**Simulation setup**:
- Applicant describes their research presentation structure first
- Coach plays committee: asks 4-6 Q&A questions after the presentation overview
- Focus on: how the applicant handles pushback, unexpected questions, and methodological challenges

**Scoring dimensions for research presentation**:
- Message: depth of research knowledge and insight
- Structure: clarity of presentation arc
- Distinctiveness: what's memorable about this applicant's research angle

### Format 3: Informal Faculty Meeting (PhD programs)

Conversation-style with a potential advisor. Not a formal interview. Applicant and faculty member discuss research interests, the applicant's background, and potential collaboration. More relaxed register.

**Simulation setup**:
- Play the target professor based on their research profile (from Application Loop)
- Questions: "Tell me about your research." "What question do you most want to pursue?" "What methods are you comfortable with?" "Why this lab specifically?"
- Duration: 15-20 minutes

**Scoring dimensions for faculty meeting**:
- Message: can the applicant explain their research clearly and show genuine intellectual engagement?
- Fit: does the applicant demonstrate specific knowledge of the professor's work?
- Authenticity: does this feel like a real conversation, or a formal performance?

### Format 4: Fellowship Interview (Fulbright country-specific, Gates Cambridge)

Varies significantly by fellowship and country. Generally: small panel, focused on the proposed project, the applicant's fit with the fellowship's mission, and readiness for the experience.

**Simulation setup**: Ask the applicant which fellowship and, if Fulbright, which country — the tone and focus vary. Simulate based on known characteristics of that specific fellowship interview.

---

## Setup Protocol

1. Ask which format and which program/fellowship
2. Pull from Application Loop: professor name (for faculty meeting), research narratives (for research presentation), concerns (for panel prep)
3. Set difficulty: calibrate to where the applicant is in their prep (not their first practice session = higher difficulty)
4. Set interviewer persona: for panel = multi-persona; for faculty meeting = specific professor; for fellowship = mission-aligned evaluator

---

## Simulation Rules

- Ask one question at a time
- Do not give feedback mid-simulation — stay in character
- Track: which narratives the applicant deploys; length of responses; energy arc over the session
- Adapt like a real interviewer: follow strong threads with natural follow-ups; redirect weak threads
- Include at least one unexpected or challenging question per simulation (a curveball)
- For panel: simulate an occasional terse or skeptical interviewer persona

---

## Post-Simulation Debrief

**Step 1**: Ask the applicant to self-assess before any feedback:
> "Before I give you my assessment — what did you feel strongest about? Where did you struggle?"

**Step 2**: Score each response briefly (not the full 5-dimension rubric — focus on 2-3 most relevant dimensions per question).

**Step 3**: Deliver the Interviewer's Read for the session:
> "From the other side of the table: [what this applicant communicated about themselves through the full arc of the interview — not just individual answers but the overall impression]"

**Step 4**: Identify 1-2 specific improvements for the next session. Do not try to fix everything at once.

**Step 5**: Offer a redo on the weakest answer:
> "Your response to [question] was the weakest. Want to try it again with a different approach?"

---

## Admit Signal → Fellowship/Program Signal

For simulated scholarship interviews, the Hire Signal is replaced by:
- **Strong Advance**: Would receive an offer from this fellowship/program based on this interview performance
- **Advance**: Would likely advance; minor weaknesses won't be decisive
- **Borderline**: Could go either way; specific issues would need to be addressed
- **No Advance**: Significant gaps that would likely prevent advancement
