# concerns — Committee Concern Anticipation

**Purpose**: Anticipate what an admissions committee will flag about this application, rank by severity, and build counter-strategies so the applicant can address them in documents and interviews.

---

## Sequence

1. Ask the applicant what concerns they expect the committee to have about their application.
2. Add concerns they may not have thought of, drawn from the Academic Profile and Application Loop data.
3. Rank by severity.
4. For each concern, build a counter-strategy — how to address it in the SoP, CV, or LoRs.

---

## Data Sources for Concerns

Pull from:
- **Academic Profile**: GPA gaps, missing publications, weak research experience, field-switch without bridge narrative, employment gap, language barriers
- **Application Loop for this program**: Fit verdict gaps, credential mismatches vs. stated program minimums, single-professor match (risky if that professor isn't taking students)
- **Program Intelligence**: What does this program value that the applicant lacks?

---

## Concern Types

**Dealbreaker concerns** (committee may reject outright if unaddressed):
- GPA below program's stated minimum
- Missing required credential (e.g., applying to a STEM PhD without any lab experience)
- Research interests don't match any active faculty
- No research experience whatsoever for a research-focused PhD

**Significant concerns** (committee will notice; must be mitigated):
- GPA below program median (but above minimum)
- No publications when most applicants have 1-2
- Field switch without a clear bridge narrative
- Long gap between degree and application with unclear explanation
- Only one viable faculty match

**Minor concerns** (committee may note; addressable with framing):
- Limited conference/poster presentations
- Research experience in adjacent (not identical) subfield
- International applicant without US academic context
- Grades improving over time but weak early years

---

## Concern Output Format

For each concern:

```
### Concern: [Title]
**Severity**: Dealbreaker / Significant / Minor
**What the committee sees**: [How a reviewer would frame this concern]
**Counter-strategy**:
  - In the SoP: [what to say and where]
  - In the CV: [what to emphasize or reframe]
  - Via LoRs: [what to ask recommenders to address]
  - At interview (if applicable): [how to respond if raised directly]
**Confidence**: [HIGH / MEDIUM — how likely is this to be raised?]
```

---

## Specific Counter-Strategies by Concern Type

**Below-median GPA**:
- SoP: Don't apologize — reframe. "My early GPA reflects [circumstance]. Since [turning point], I have [evidence of improvement / research performance]." Lead with research capability.
- CV: Ensure research accomplishments are prominent — publications, presentations, concrete contributions
- LoRs: Ask advisors to explicitly address research capability independent of GPA. "If your GPA is a concern, I'd appreciate it if you could speak to your direct observation of my research ability and potential."

**No publications**:
- SoP: Describe research experience with specificity — methods, contributions, outcomes — to demonstrate research capability even without a publication record
- LoRs: Ask the research supervisor to address why you haven't published yet (was the work submitted? is it in preparation? was this a learning experience?) and explicitly assess your potential
- Note: For many PhD programs, particularly at the Master's-to-PhD level, publications are a differentiator not a requirement. Frame accordingly.

**Field switch**:
- The bridge narrative must appear explicitly in the SoP — not as an apology but as a strength
- Frame the prior field as providing a distinctive perspective: "My background in [A] gives me tools and questions that most [B] researchers lack"
- Ask one LoR from the prior field to validate the connection

**Employment gap**:
- Address it directly in a brief SoP sentence: "After completing my degree in [year], I [what I did]. This experience [what it contributed]."
- Don't over-explain. One clear sentence is better than a paragraph of justification.

**Reapplicant (applying after previous rejection)**:
- Committees know. Don't hide it. Be explicit about what changed: new research experience, clearer research direction, stronger LoRs, revised SoP.
- The SoP should implicitly demonstrate growth since the last application without stating "I was rejected before."
- If you received feedback: address it directly. If you didn't: that's fine.

After generating concerns and counter-strategies, offer:

> "Want to draft a specific section to address [the highest-severity concern] right now? We could work on the SoP passage or prepare a counter-argument for the program-fit paragraph."
